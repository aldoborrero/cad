# ODrive v3.5 Weakness Audit (24V Variant)

**Audience:** the v4 design engineer.
**Scope note:** the audited schematic PDF (`schematic_v3.5.pdf`, 4 sheets: Top / MotorCell M0 / MotorCell_noPow M1 / AuxHalfH) is the **24V variant**. Voltage-headroom findings (30V FETs, ~6V margin) are stated for this variant; the 56V board shifts which component fails first but shares most structural weaknesses. Findings draw on the schematic, `CHANGELOG.md`, board photos (`v3.5_top.PNG` / `v3.5_bottom.PNG`), and documented ODrive v3.x field-failure reports.

## Executive Summary

ODrive v3.5 is a competent, iterated design whose control and measurement core is fundamentally sound, but its protection architecture is close to nonexistent: the DC input has no fuse, no reverse-polarity protection, and no inrush limiting into a 3.76mF bulk bank; the only overvoltage defense for 30V FETs on a 24V bus is a firmware ADC loop plus an *optional* external brake resistor; every external connector (USB, CAN, encoders, SWD) lacks ESD/transient protection; and the entire logic supply — including the SPI path that configures both gate drivers — hangs off one gate driver's integrated buck, so the single most common field failure (shorted M0 FET killing U4) takes down all supervision while the unfused bus stays live. The 24V variant compounds this with obsolete 30V NTMFS4935N FETs leaving ~6V of regen headroom, which is the classic v3.x board-killer. The audit confirms **10 critical, 21 major, and 18 minor** findings. v4 should be built around three structural moves: a protected power entry (e-fuse/ideal-diode + TVS), a hardware overvoltage backstop independent of the MCU, and an independent system power rail with 40V+ FETs on a current-production gate driver (DRV8301 is NRND).

## 1. DC Bus Input & Power Entry

### bus-input-1 — No fuse or overcurrent protection anywhere on the DC bus input (critical)

**Claim.** The main power connector J9 (and parallel secondary pads J7/J8) connect straight to DCBUS and the 8x470uF bank with no fuse, e-fuse, or any series element. A shorted FET pair, brake-chopper shoot-through in the AuxHalfH stage, a swarf short, or a miswired motor lead lets the battery/PSU dump unlimited current into the board until traces vaporize or the source itself becomes the fuse — a fire hazard in plausible normal use.

**Evidence.** Sheet 1: J9 pins 1/2 go directly to DCBUS/PGND and the C11–C18 470uF bank; J7/J8 pads tap the same net. A designator search across all four sheets finds no F* part and only two diodes (D1 buck catch on p2, D2 bootstrap on p4) — no series protective component of any kind between the input and the DRV8301 power stages or the U6/Q25–Q28 brake bridge.

**Consequence.** Any bus short with a battery source has no interrupting element on the board; direct battery connection is the advertised use case.

**v4 fix.** Add board-level overcurrent protection: a bolt-down or MINI/ATO blade fuse footprint (e.g. Littelfuse 498-series holder, sized ~1.5x max continuous bus current), or better an integrated hot-swap/e-fuse controller (TI LM5069 or LM74800-Q1 driving adequate SO-8 FETs), which also solves inrush and reverse protection. At absolute minimum, a DNP fuse footprint plus documentation.

### bus-input-2 — No reverse-polarity protection on the DC input (critical)

**Claim.** Reversing supply leads at J9 forward-biases the body-diode chains of the 24 power FETs plus the brake-bridge FETs, which clamp the reversed bus at about −1.5 to −2V and carry unlimited source current with no fuse or series element to interrupt it — destroying the FET bank and PCB traces (fire hazard). The electrolytics and DRV8301s see only the ~2V clamped reversal, not full −24V, but sustained reverse current still stresses them. No reverse protection of any kind exists on the schematic.

**Evidence.** Sheet 1: J9/J7/J8 wire directly to DCBUS and PGND with polarized 470uF electrolytics C11–C18 across them; no series diode, reverse-blocking FET, or crowbar-plus-fuse anywhere in the input path (verified across sheets 1–4).

**Consequence.** Hardware destruction on a trivial user error (lead swap on an unkeyed screw terminal), compounded by bus-input-1's missing fuse.

**v4 fix.** Ideal-diode/back-to-back NFET input stage. Because the drive regenerates into the supply, use a bidirectional controller: TI LM74800-Q1 (or LM5069 + discrete back-to-back FETs) with two paralleled 40V/1–2mOhm FETs (e.g. BSC010N04LS). A cheap partial alternative: a crowbar rectifier across the bus plus the fuse from bus-input-1.

### bus-input-4 — Regen overvoltage protection is entirely firmware-dependent and relies on an optional external resistor (critical)

**Claim.** During deceleration the motors pump energy into the bus; with a current-limited PSU (which cannot sink current) the only absorber is the brake chopper — but its resistor at J23 is optional, and the chopper is driven purely by MCU firmware via U6's H/L inputs. If the resistor is absent, firmware crashes/hangs, or the board is in DFU (the design itself acknowledges DFU corrupts these lines — R59/R60 pulldowns were added for exactly that), the bus rises unchecked. The NTMFS4935N FETs are 30V parts on a 24V bus: ~6V of headroom before avalanche across 12 FET positions per motor, plus the electrolytics' rating is exceeded.

**Evidence.** Sheet 4: brake chopper U6/Q25–Q28 ("MOSFETS: NTMFS4935NT1G", 30V) with external resistor on J23/J21/J22; H/L come from MCU nets AUX_H/AUX_L. Sheet 1: R59/R60 3.3k pulldowns annotated "Strong pull-downs required to overcome STM32 weak pull-up activated in DFU mode" — proof the chopper inputs are MCU-vulnerable. Changelog v3.5: C1 changed 2.2uF→270pF "for cycle by cycle Vbus check", i.e. OV response is a software loop. No TVS, hardware comparator, or clamp exists on any sheet.

**Consequence.** Hardware destruction in a completely normal use case: PSU-powered bench setup with aggressive decel.

**v4 fix.** Hardware overvoltage backstop independent of the MCU: a comparator (TLV7011/TLV3691 + TL431 or divider off DCBUS) that (a) asserts the brake low-side gate directly and (b) pulls DRV8301 EN_GATE low, latching until VBUS falls. Populate a default on-board brake resistor (even a small 2–5W chassis part) so the safe state exists without user action; add the bus TVS from bus-input-5 as a last-ditch clamp.

### system-1 — No hardware OVP on DCBUS; regen relies entirely on optional external brake resistor plus firmware (critical)

**Claim.** The only overvoltage defense is firmware reading VBUS_S through the R6(10k)/R7(1k)/C1(270pF) divider into the MCU ADC. There is no TVS, crowbar, or hardware comparator anywhere on the bus. With 30V FETs and ~6V headroom, a missing/disconnected/misconfigured brake resistor, a broken brake wire, or a firmware fault state (DFU, hard fault, watchdog reset mid-decel) lets the 8x470uF bank pump past 30V and avalanche all 24+ FETs. This is the classic ODrive v3.x board-killer failure mode.

**Evidence.** Page 1: VBUS_S divider into the MCU only; DCBUS bulk = C11–C18; no TVS or clamp on any sheet (only diodes: D1 buck catch, D2 bootstrap). Page 4: brake drive is U6 half-bridge to screw terminal J23, resistor external and optional. Page 2: NTMFS4935NT1G (30V) on the 24V bus. Changelog confirms C1 resize for a cycle-by-cycle firmware Vbus check.

**Consequence.** Loss of the dump path during regen plausibly destroys the entire power stage; matches widely documented community failure reports.

**v4 fix.** Layered hardware OVP independent of firmware: (1) bus TVS sized under the FET rating directly across DCBUS/PGND near J9; (2) hardware OV comparator (TLV7031 + REF3312 off the divider) that forces EN_GATE low and/or the brake low-side on, bypassing the MCU; (3) 40V FETs (e.g. NVMFS5C628NL, ~2mOhm) to restore regen headroom; (4) brake-resistor presence/continuity detection (small sense current read by a spare ADC pin) so firmware refuses closed-loop until a dump path exists.

### system-2 — DC input: no fuse, no reverse protection, no inrush limiting into 3.8mF (critical)

**Claim.** Consolidated input-protection finding: J9 plus aux pads J7/J8 (added v3.3) tie directly to DCBUS with a 3.76mF nominal bulk bank and zero series elements.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J9/J7/J8 → DCBUS with C11–C18 to PGND; no fuse, diode, FET, or NTC on any sheet. `v3.5_top.PNG`: only small "DC" silk with thin polarity glyphs at the input terminals.

**Consequence.** Reverse polarity destroys the board through the FET body-diode path; a downstream short on a battery supply has no disconnect; hot-plug arcs at the terminals.

**v4 fix.** (1) Fuse or fuse footprint in series with DC+ (MINI blade clip or Littelfuse NANO2 885 series per variant); (2) ideal-diode reverse protection (LM74700-Q1 + 40V NMOS such as NVMFS5C628NL, ~20mV drop at load); (3) inrush handling — NTC bypassed by the ideal-diode FET after precharge, or a documented precharge resistor; (4) enlarged, unambiguous "+"/"−" silkscreen at J9.

### web-1 — Field-confirmed: reversed DC input instantly destroys the board (critical)

**Claim.** ODrive v3.x has no reverse-polarity protection; a reversed battery or supply connection instantly destroys the board.

**Evidence.** Field reports (ODrive forum #8428): a reversed battery on a v3.6 released "magic smoke immediately"; another user saw a big spark on a reversed XT-60 and lost USB. Schematic page 1 confirms the topology: no series element of any kind between the input and the electrolytics/FET stages.

**Consequence.** Confirms bus-input-2 with fielded failures — this is not theoretical.

**v4 fix.** Back-to-back P-FET or ideal-diode controller (LM74700/LM5069 class) rated for full bus current; at minimum a keyed connector plus input fuse and reverse-blocking TVS so a reversed hookup blows the fuse instead of the board.

### web-2 — Field-confirmed: un-dumped regen energy avalanches the FETs (critical)

**Claim.** With no hardware bus clamp and a firmware-driven chopper requiring an optional resistor, un-dumped braking energy (resistor absent/misconfigured, or supply/BMS blocking regen) drives the bus into Vds avalanche of the FETs, destroying the power stage. On the 24V variant the 30V FETs leave only ~6V of headroom.

**Evidence.** GitHub issue #77 "Robustify regen handling" (project author): un-dumped regeneration is one of the few things that will "for sure blow up the FETs" — with a non-sinking supply the energy can only exit via Vds breakdown. Field threads report DRV chips and FETs permanently damaged after regen events where a BMS blocked reverse current. Protection today is firmware-only (`dc_bus_overvoltage_trip_level`).

**Consequence.** Power-stage destruction in plausible normal use (regen into a non-sinking supply).

**v4 fix.** Bus TVS/clamp sized above max operating voltage but below FET Vds rating; analog comparator that hardware-disables the gate driver and fires the brake chopper; FETs with real Vds headroom.

### bus-input-3 — No inrush limiting or precharge into ~3.8mF of bulk capacitance (major)

**Claim.** Connecting a live 24V battery to J9 charges C11–C18 (3.76mF) plus ~24uF of ceramics through nothing but wiring resistance. Peak inrush of hundreds of amps causes connector arcing and pitting at J9, alarming sparks, screw-terminal contact erosion, ESR stress on the electrolytics, and voltage dips that can reset other nodes sharing the battery.

**Evidence.** Sheet 1: bulk bank C11–C18 and ceramics C26/C40/C44/C55/C68 (4.7uF) sit directly across J9 with no series NTC, precharge resistor, relay, or soft-start element. Hot-connection sparking is well-documented real-world ODrive v3 behavior.

**Consequence.** Reliability/contact-erosion risk; not by itself destructive.

**v4 fix.** If the LM74800/LM5069 e-fuse from bus-input-1/2 is adopted, its dV/dt-controlled soft start (gate capacitor) solves precharge — one part closes all three findings. Standalone alternative: an inrush NTC (Ametherm SL32 2R025 class) with an optional bypass FET/relay closed by firmware after VBUS_S reads stable.

### web-6 — Externally driven motor back-feeds the bus through FET body diodes (major)

**Claim.** A motor spun externally rectifies back-EMF through the MOSFET body diodes onto the DC bus, which has no clamp of any kind; the DRV8301s sit directly on the bus (PVDD1) and the brake chopper requires firmware plus an optional resistor. On 56V boards the DRV8301 is the documented casualty; on this 24V variant the 30V FETs avalanche first. Either way, unclamped back-driven overvoltage can destroy bus-connected silicon.

**Evidence.** Forum thread #13093: multiple units developed persistent DRV_FAULT surviving power cycles, requiring DRV chip replacement; attributed to rectified back-EMF with the BMS blocking regen or cables disconnected. Schematic confirms PVDD1 (pin 29, U4/U5) directly on DCBUS and no clamp anywhere.

**Consequence.** An externally moved motor with the supply off or blocked can destroy the power stage or drivers.

**v4 fix.** Clamp the bus in hardware (TVS sized for the driver's abs-max), select parts with headroom over worst-case back-driven EMF, and consider a brake-chopper/crowbar path that works whenever bus voltage exists, not only when firmware runs.

### bus-input-5 — No TVS or transient clamp on DCBUS (minor)

**Claim.** No TVS exists on DCBUS, but with 3.76mF of bulk directly on the bus, lead-inductance spikes are absorbed to ~1V excursions and cannot avalanche the FET bank; and the marginal 30V-FET-on-24V-bus headroom cannot be fixed by a TVS at all, since any ≥24V-standoff device clamps above 30V. A TVS (plus connector-local ceramics) is a robustness improvement against hot-plug ring and external surges only; the real fixes are FET voltage headroom and the hardware regen backstop of bus-input-4.

**Evidence.** Sheet 1: DCBUS carries only C11–C18/ceramics — no TVS designator on sheets 1–4. Sheet 4 note confirms 30V-rated switches on the 24V bus.

**Consequence.** Reduced robustness against hot-plug ring and external surge; not an operative failure mode on its own.

**v4 fix.** High-energy unidirectional TVS directly across J9: SMDJ26A (Vwm 26V, clamps ~42V — pair with 40V FETs) or, if keeping 30V FETs, SMCJ24A understanding it only covers short transients; footprint-share an SMDJ58A for the 56V variant. Add a 100nF + 2.2uF ceramic pair at the connector for HF snubbing.

## 2. Power Stage & Gate Drive

### power-stage-1 — 30V FETs leave ~6V headroom on a 24V bus with regen and no bus clamp (critical)

**Claim.** All 24 bridge FETs plus the 4 brake FETs (Q1–Q28, "MOSFETS: NTMFS4935NT1G" on sheets 2–4) are 30V parts on a 24V DCBUS. Regen pumps the bus above 24V (especially with a non-sinking PSU and the optional brake resistor unfitted), and switch-node/bus ringing adds several volts on top; nothing clamps it. A single regen event or supply-lead inductance spike (hot-plug LC ring tends toward 2x supply) can avalanche the FETs and destroy the power stage. The NTMFS4935N is also end-of-life at onsemi, blocking a v4 build regardless.

**Evidence.** Sheets 2/3/4: DCBUS rail feeding Q1–Q28 drains with the 30V-part annotation on each sheet; no TVS or snubber anywhere; brake resistor on J23 optional. onsemi lists NTMFS4935N as Obsolete. This is the documented field-failure mode of ODrive v3.x 24V boards.

**Consequence.** Destroys hardware in plausible normal use; firmware OV trip cannot absorb the energy.

**v4 fix.** 40V FETs sized for bus + margin: onsemi NVMFS5C628NL (40V, 1.6mOhm, SO-8FL drop-in class) or TI CSD18540Q5B. Add an SMDJ26A TVS across DCBUS–PGND near the power connector, and make the brake/OV chopper path default-populated protection, not optional.

### power-stage-5 — Brake half-bridge driver U6: no shoot-through interlock, bootstrap-only high side (major)

**Claim.** U6 is a plain dual-input half-bridge driver (independent HI pin 2 / LI pin 3, no cross-conduction interlock or dead-time). If firmware, a crashed MCU, or the DFU-mode weak pull-ups (mitigated only by external pull-downs) ever assert H and L together, Q25/Q26 and Q27/Q28 short DCBUS with nothing in hardware to stop it. The high side — the switch that actually applies the brake resistor (J23 pin 2 = switch node HS, pin 1 = PGND) across the bus — is bootstrap-supplied (D2 + C59 100nF only): it cannot be held on continuously, delivers no braking until a low-side pulse first charges the bootstrap, and a stuck-high failure degrades into an undriven gate rather than a defined state.

**Evidence.** Sheet 4: U6 VDD/HI/LI/VSS/HB/HO/HS/LO with H/L driven directly from MCU GPIO (PB11/PB10); D2 + C59 bootstrap; no gate-source pull-downs on Q25–Q28 (only R51/R54 series). Changelog v3.5: "Strong pull-down resistors on the control signals to the AUX half-H to counteract weak pullups activated in DFU mode" — the hazard class acknowledged, patched only at the inputs.

**Consequence.** Simultaneous H+L from a firmware fault shorts DCBUS through the brake bridge; requires a fault to manifest, hence major not critical.

**v4 fix.** A half-bridge driver with input interlock and fixed dead-time (UCC27712-class interlock behavior, or a low-voltage MP1907/NCP81074-class part), plus 10k–100k gate-source pull-downs directly on Q25–Q28. If continuous 100% braking duty is wanted, pick a driver with a trickle/charge-pump high side, or restructure so the low-side switch drives the resistor (resistor from DCBUS to switch node).

### power-stage-6 — Brake driver powered from the DRV8301's GVDD regulator; GVDD is a global power net (major)

**Claim.** U6's VDD (C61 2.2uF) and bootstrap diode D2 hang on GVDD — the DRV8301's internal ~11V gate-drive regulator output, which TI specifies for internal gate-drive use and decoupling only. Brake gate charge and bootstrap refresh drawn from GVDD can pull it down during simultaneous motor switching and trip GVDD_UV, shutting down the motor driver. Worse, GVDD is drawn as a power port on sheets 2, 3, and 4; Altium power ports are global, so as drawn the two DRV8301s' internal regulators are paralleled onto one net.

**Evidence.** Sheet 2: GVDD port at U4 pin 13 (C32 2.2uF); sheet 3: U5 pin 13 (C47 2.2uF); sheet 4: GVDD feeding U6 VDD, D2 anode, C61. The Aux sheet symbol exposes only H/L/TEMP ports, so U6's supply can only arrive via the global net. Caveat: the external load is a few mA and fielded boards tolerate it — low end of major; paralleled regulators typically resolve as one device sourcing the load rather than damage.

**Consequence.** GVDD_UV trip disables motor drive mid-operation; out-of-spec use of the driver's internal rail.

**v4 fix.** Power the brake gate driver from its own supply (small 12V rail, e.g. LMR51410 buck from DCBUS, or a brake driver fed from 5V/12V). Keep each DRV's GVDD strictly local (net labels GVDD_M0/GVDD_M1, not global power ports). DRV8353 makes this moot — its VGLS/charge-pump rails are explicitly internal.

### system-3 — Fault coordination degraded: nOCTW unconnected, nFAULT wired-OR, single shared EN_GATE (major)

**Claim.** nOCTW on both DRV8301s is explicitly no-connected, both nFAULT lines are wired-OR into PD2, and one shared EN_GATE serves both drivers. Firmware gets no OT/OC warning tier, cannot attribute a trip without SPI reads, and cannot disable or reset one axis's driver without power-cycling both charge pumps. Note the DRV8301's internal OC shutdown/OTSD still act in hardware regardless; and because VDS-sensed trip current falls as Rdson rises with temperature, a hot FET trips at *lower* current — the un-monitored warning tier mainly costs early notice and causes unattributable nuisance trips rather than missed protection.

**Evidence.** Page 1: both MotorCell sheet symbols show nOCTW with a no-connect X; both nFAULT ports on one net into U2A pin 54 (PD2); a single EN_GATE net (U2A pin 33) feeds both blocks. Page 2: U4 nOCTW/nFAULT (pins 5/6) with pull-ups but only nFAULT leaves the sheet.

**Consequence.** Slow fault attribution, no per-axis disable, no early-warning tier.

**v4 fix.** Per-driver EN_GATE and nFAULT to separate EXTI-capable pins; route both nOCTW lines to MCU inputs (pins freed by the removed DC_CAL signals). Optionally a small hardware interlock — nFAULT ANDed into the INHx lines (74LVC1G08-class) — so a latched driver fault forces PWM-off within propagation delay.

### web-3 — DRV8301 SH_x power-up errata: driver malfunctions if the motor is spinning at boot (major)

**Claim.** The DRV8301 has a community/TI-E2E-documented power-up erratum (not in the published datasheet errata): gate drivers may malfunction (spurious FETLA_OC, DRV_FAULT) if significant back-EMF (~>8.5V per community reports) is present on any SH_x pin when EN_GATE is asserted — i.e. when the motor is spinning at power-up or reset. v3.5 has no hardware mitigation: SH_x pins connect directly to the phase nodes and EN_GATE comes straight from MCU GPIO PB12.

**Evidence.** Forum thread "Workaround for DRV8301 errata" (#9725): boards booting with rotating motors (wind turbines, hydro, back-drivable joints) throw spurious faults; no v3.x hardware workaround exists; ODrive Pro/S1 escaped it by moving to DRV8353. Schematic confirms no bleed resistors or sequencing hardware.

**Consequence.** Functional/reliability failure in a realistic use class (back-drivable joints, turbines).

**v4 fix.** Use a driver without the erratum (DRV8353/DRV8353R or discrete half-bridge drivers), and/or power-up sequencing that keeps phase voltage bled below threshold (bleed resistors / delayed EN_GATE) before enabling.

### web-4 — GVDD ripple trips DRV undervoltage above ~40–45A (v3.4 errata) (major)

**Claim.** Official v3.4 errata: high phase switching current couples ripple into the DRV8301 GVDD supply (its decoupling was referenced to PGND, in the shunt/switching return path), tripping GVDD undervoltage and shutting down both motors above ~45A on M0; documented workaround was a 40A limit. v3.5 fixed this during its design cycle — repo commit 3c5231c "Move GVDD caps from PGND to GND" (Jan 2018) plus the 4-layer split ground planes. The v4 lesson: keep gate-supply decoupling out of power-return paths from the start.

**Evidence.** ODrive troubleshooting docs (v3.4 DRV faults at 45–50A, 40A derate workaround); this repo's commit history; v3.5 schematic shows GVDD decoupling referenced to GND.

**Consequence.** A documented v3.x grounding defect; fixed in v3.5 but a standing lesson for v4 layout.

**v4 fix.** Low-inductance gate-supply decoupling referenced to the quiet ground (never PGND shunt paths), star-separated power/signal grounds under the driver, bulk + ceramic close to GVDD so full rated phase current cannot brown out the driver.

### web-5 — DRV8301 is NRND/legacy — blocks a new design (major)

**Claim.** The central gate driver + shunt-amp + buck part of v3.x, TI DRV8301, is not recommended for new designs; ODrive itself lists v3.6 as NRND and moved Pro/S1 to DRV8353. Carrying it into v4 inherits supply risk plus the web-3 power-up erratum.

**Evidence.** TI product page marks DRV8301 legacy/NRND with DRV8353 as successor; official ODrive shop lists "ODrive v3.6 (NRND)". Schematic confirms it is the load-bearing part: gate drive, both CSAs, SPI config, and (U4) the integrated buck generating the whole 5V rail.

**Consequence.** Component obsolescence blocks a v4 build on this BOM.

**v4 fix.** Design around DRV8353RS (or an equivalent current-production driver): no spinning-start erratum, adjustable gate drive current (IDRIVE), more flexible CSA gain, active roadmap. Note the DRV8353**RS** variant retains an integrated buck; regardless, provide system power independently (see power-rails-1).

### web-7 — FETs fail short at stall/high current; no protection layer beyond DRV8301 OC sensing (major)

**Claim.** v3.x FETs (NTMFS4935N) have been reported failing drain-source shorted under stall/high commanded current (notably at ~90A limits); the only overcurrent protection is the DRV8301's internal VDS sensing plus firmware acting on the two phase shunt amplifiers — no fuse, no independent hardware trip, FET temperature sensed only via a board NTC read by firmware. A single OC/thermal event can destroy the half-bridge and sometimes the driver.

**Evidence.** Field reports (forum #9805): NTMFS4935NT1G "damaged with a short between Drain to Source" after 90A limits; FETs "only cook when the motor is stalled at maximum current"; cracked driver ICs alongside failed FETs; overheating threads show marginal thermal design without heatsinking. Pages 2/3 confirm the protection stack: VDS sensing + two shunts (B/C legs only, no bus current sense) + firmware NTC, nothing else.

**Consequence.** Real reliability risk at hard but user-reachable operating points.

**v4 fix.** FETs with real thermal margin at rated stall current plus a heatsink/thermal-via strategy; DRV8353 programmable Vds trip set properly per FET choice; phase/bus current sensing supporting a fast hardware trip; an on-board FET thermistor in the trip path.

### power-stage-3 — One shared 2.2R gate resistor per paralleled FET pair (minor)

**Claim.** Every paralleled FET pair shares a single 2.2R gate resistor with gates hard-tied (R17–R19/R22–R24 on M0, R36–R38/R40–R42 on M1, R51/R54 on the brake bridge), providing no differential damping of the inter-gate resonant loop. This can allow unequal dynamic sharing and ringing that erodes the 30V margin, but it is not an independently demonstrated failure mode on fielded v3.x hardware — it compounds power-stage-1 rather than standing alone.

**Evidence.** Sheet 2: R17→Q1/Q2, R18→Q3/Q4, R19→Q5/Q6, R22–R24 low-side pairs; sheet 3 mirrors (R36–R42/Q13–Q24); sheet 4: R51→Q25/Q26, R54→Q27/Q28. No per-gate resistors anywhere. The mechanism is documented in vendor app notes.

**Consequence.** Robustness deficit; standard best practice not followed.

**v4 fix.** Per-FET gate resistors placed at each gate pin (2x 4.7R replacing each shared 2.2R keeps similar net drive). With DRV8353, use IDRIVE current control plus small per-gate resistors, and keep the paralleled pair's gate/source routing symmetric.

### power-stage-4 — Only phases B/C carry shunts; phase A reconstructed; DC_CAL routing removed (minor)

**Claim.** Each bridge has shunts only on the B and C legs (R27/R28, R45/R46, 500uOhm, kelvin net-ties); phase A is reconstructed as −(Ib+Ic), so CSA errors add and there is no redundant measurement — a limitation inherent to the DRV8301's two integrated CSAs and standard two-shunt FOC. The v3.5 removal of DC_CAL pin routing only removes the pin-triggered calibration path; the same offset calibration remains available at runtime via SPI (DC_CAL_CH1/CH2 bits), and phase-A leg faults are still caught by the DRV8301's VDS overcurrent sensing.

**Evidence.** Sheet 2: R27/R28 with net-ties NT8/NT2, NT9/NT3 into SN1/SP1, SN2/SP2; SL_A has no shunt. Sheet 3: R45/R46, NT10/NT4, NT11/NT5. CHANGELOG v3.5 "Removed: DC_CAL signals"; U4 pin 12 no longer MCU-controlled.

**Consequence.** No averaging/redundancy, no phase-A firmware current telemetry; a modest measurement limitation.

**v4 fix.** Populate all three legs with shunts and use the DRV8353's three integrated CSAs (with internal SPI auto-offset-calibration, restoring what DC_CAL removal lost). Keep the kelvin net-tie scheme on all three shunts.

### power-stage-7 — No snubbers or per-half-bridge HF bus decoupling (minor)

**Claim.** No RC snubber footprints exist on any switch node (motor or brake), and the motor-cell sheets contain no DCBUS–PGND decoupling at the FET half-bridges — HF bus decoupling is limited to five 4.7uF ceramics (C26/C40/C44/C55/C68) drawn alongside the bulk bank on the top sheet plus 68nF at each DRV8301 PVDD1. Commutation-loop inductance therefore depends entirely on where layout placed those five ceramics.

**Evidence.** Sheet 2: only C25 68nF + C60 4.7uF (PVDD2) and bootstrap caps near the bridge; no cap from DCBUS to PGND at Q1–Q12 and no RC across any SH_x/SL_x node. Sheets 3/4 identical pattern.

**Consequence.** Untunable ringing behavior; layout-dependent commutation loop.

**v4 fix.** Per-half-bridge ceramic decoupling (2x 2.2uF 50V X7R directly across each leg drain-to-PGND) and RC snubber footprints (start ~1R + 1nF, tune on hardware) across each low-side FET and the brake switch node — even unpopulated, footprints make v4 tunable.

### power-stage-8 — 100nF bootstrap caps marginal for two paralleled FETs per switch (minor)

**Claim.** Bootstrap caps C30/C31/C34 (M0), C45/C46/C49 (M1), C59 (brake) are 100nF — the DRV8301 datasheet's single-FET recommendation — but every high-side switch is two NTMFS4935N in parallel, roughly doubling gate charge per cycle (2x ~30nC gives ~0.6–0.7V droop per switching event) on top of driver quiescent draw during long high-side on-times at low modulation. That thins high-side enhancement exactly when Rds(on) and heating matter most. Works in practice, but with less margin than needed.

**Evidence.** Sheet 2: C30/C31/C34 = 100nF on BST_A/B/C of U4 driving pairs Q1/Q2, Q3/Q4, Q5/Q6; sheet 3: C45/C46/C49; sheet 4: C59 for Q25/Q26.

**Consequence.** Reduced high-side enhancement margin at low modulation depth.

**v4 fix.** 220–470nF X7R 25V bootstraps (raising GVDD decoupling to 4.7uF to keep the reservoir ratio), verified against the actual Qg of the chosen 40V FETs.

## 3. Sensing & Feedback

### sensing-3 — VBUS_S: the board's only OV-protection input is an unbuffered 909R divider, 650kHz corner, no clamp (major)

**Claim.** The unbuffered R6/R7 (10K/1k) divider with C1 = 270pF (anti-alias corner ~650kHz) feeding the MCU ADC directly is the board's only overvoltage-protection input (no TVS, fuse, or hardware OV comparator; brake resistor optional): switching noise passes into the cycle-by-cycle trip decision, accuracy depends silently on ADC sample-time configuration against the 909R source, and there is no clamp on VBUS_S — though the 10k series resistance limits ESD-diode injection to a few mA, so the risk is a compromised trip decision rather than pin destruction.

**Evidence.** Page 1: DCBUS → R6 10K → VBUS_S → R7 1k ∥ C1 270pF → AGND, direct to a U2 ADC pin with no buffer or clamp. CHANGELOG v3.5: "Change C1 from 2.2uF to 270pF for cycle by cycle Vbus check". Page 4: brake output optional external. Full scale ≈ 36.3V at 3.3V.

**Consequence.** A noisy, unclamped, sample-time-sensitive node is the sole input to the only overvoltage defense on a board with ~6V FET headroom.

**v4 fix.** Buffer the divider (half a TLV9062 from AVCC) into the ADC through 100R/1nF, restoring a proper two-stage filter (~50kHz fast pole for the trip path) without sample-time sensitivity; BAT54S clamp from VBUS_S to AVCC/AGND; and back the firmware check with independent hardware OVP — bus TVS plus a comparator on VBUS_S wired to EN_GATE/nFAULT.

### sensing-4 — No motor thermistor input; open on-board NTC fails silently as "cold" (major)

**Claim.** All three temperature channels (M0_TEMP, M1_TEMP, AUX_TEMP) are on-board 10k NTCs measuring the PCB near the FETs; there is no conditioned input for a motor winding thermistor, so the component with the slowest, most damaging thermal failure mode — the motor — is unmonitored. Additionally, with the NTC from AVCC and a fixed 3.3k to AGND, an open NTC or cracked joint reads ~0V, i.e. "very cold", disabling overtemperature protection in the no-warning direction unless firmware treats near-zero as a sensor fault.

**Evidence.** Page 2: R12 10k NTC from AVCC, R13 3.3k + C23 2.2uF to AGND → TEMP; page 3: R31/R35/C43; page 4: R49/R50/C58. Page 1: J3/J4 expose GPIOs/encoder pins but no thermistor-ready divider/filter/ESD input (v3.5's extra ADC-capable GPIO is a bare pin).

**Consequence.** Motor burnout risk in plausible normal use; FET sensing fails silent-cold.

**v4 fix.** One dedicated motor-thermistor pin per axis on the encoder connectors: 10k pull-up to AVCC, 1k series + 100nF to AGND, ESD clamp (PESD3V3L1BA), routed to spare ADC channels. In firmware, treat readings near both rails as sensor faults; choose divider polarity (or a plausibility window) so open-circuit fails toward "hot/fault", not "cold".

### sensing-5 — Brake (AUX) leg has zero feedback (major)

**Claim.** v3.5 removed the AUX current-sense amplifier (v3.3 had already removed AUX_V voltage measurement), leaving the brake half-bridge completely open-loop: firmware cannot detect a missing/wrong-value brake resistor, a shorted or stuck-on brake FET (Q27/Q28), or an overloaded resistor. Since the brake path is the intended absorber of regen energy on a board with no TVS, an undetected brake-path failure converts directly into a bus overvoltage event, and a stuck-on FET across DCBUS cooks the resistor or FETs with no fault signal — the DRV8301's OC protection does not cover U6's FETs.

**Evidence.** Page 4: U6, Q25–Q28, D2, R51/R54 — no shunt, no CSA, no sense divider anywhere on the sheet; only the NTC nearby. CHANGELOG v3.5 "Removed: AUX current sense amplifier"; v3.3 "Removed: AUX_V voltage measurement no longer available".

**Consequence.** Brake failures are invisible until they escalate to bus overvoltage (compounding system-1).

**v4 fix.** Minimal brake telemetry: 2mOhm low-side shunt in the Q27/Q28 source leg with an INA180A2 into a spare ADC channel (stuck-on detection, resistor-presence check at first pulse, I2t protection), or at minimum a divider on the brake output node so firmware can verify the half-bridge actually switches.

### system-4 — Brake half-bridge: no current sensing, no fault feedback (major)

**Claim.** Companion to sensing-5 with the terminal topology corrected: the brake resistor hangs between the switch node (AUX "+", J23 pin 2/J21) and PGND (J23 pin 1/J22) — the "+" terminal is the switch node, not permanently-live DCBUS; it reaches DCBUS when the high-side pair conducts (or transiently via Q25/Q26 body diodes). Dumping turns on the high-side pair, so a shorted or undersized resistor puts an unmeasured short across DCBUS bounded only by FET destruction, and a failed-open resistor silently defeats regen protection. The DFU weak-pull-up hazard patched by R59/R60 shows the leg can be actuated outside firmware control.

**Evidence.** CHANGELOG line 27; page 4 topology; page 1 R59/R60 with the DFU note; `v3.5_top.PNG` shows the AUX terminal beside the phase outputs.

**Consequence.** Same escalation chain as sensing-5; AUX_TEMP is the only telemetry.

**v4 fix.** Low-side shunt + INA181A2 into a spare ADC pin, enabling resistor-open detection before closed-loop enable, short detection with fast PWM cutoff, and dissipated-power accounting. Alternatively a smart low-side switch with built-in current limit/fault flag (BTS3xxx family) for the dump path; label the AUX "+" terminal clearly on silkscreen.

### sensing-1 — SO1/SO2 filter pole set by unspecified CSA output impedance (minor)

**Claim.** The v3.2 fix for the v3.1 current-sense filter bug resized the caps only: v3.5 still has R26/R29 = 22R with C38/C39 = 2200pF (U4) and R44/R47 = 22R with C53/C54 = 2200pF (U5). Because the DRV8301 datasheet does not specify CSA output impedance, the actual anti-alias corner (nominally 3.3MHz from 22R alone; likely ~0.6–1MHz including amp Zo) is uncontrolled and part-to-part variable. PWM-center-synchronized sampling mitigates most switching-edge aliasing in practice, so this is a measurement-robustness weakness rather than a functional defect.

**Evidence.** Page 2: R26/R29 in series with U4 pins 25/26 (SO1/SO2), C38/C39 to AGND at TP6/TP7; page 3 mirrors on U5. CHANGELOG v3.2: "Fix current sensor filter capacitor values" — only the caps were ever resized.

**Consequence.** Noise/corner variability in the current measurement, board to board.

**v4 fix.** Remove the dependence on CSA Zo: (a) external CSAs (INA240A2) with a designed 1k/1nF anti-alias filter, (b) a driver with specified CSA drive (DRV8320RS/DRV8353RS) and R ≥ 10x its Zo, or (c) buffer SO1/SO2 with a TLV9062 and a deliberate ~100kHz two-pole filter matched to the ADC sample point.

### sensing-2 — 500uOhm two-leg shunts: ~40mA/LSB, offset-dominated at low current (minor)

**Claim.** 500uOhm shunts on only the B/C low-side legs (phase A ties directly to PGND) give ~40mA/LSB at the default gain-40 setting and only 5mV of shunt signal at 10A, so CSA offset and thermal EMF dominate low-current torque accuracy, and phase A must always be reconstructed, degrading current sampling at high modulation index. These are deliberate tradeoffs of the two-CSA DRV8301 (gain is SPI-selectable up to 80, partially mitigating resolution) — a measurement-quality limitation, not a reliability defect.

**Evidence.** Page 2: R27/R28 500uOhm, kelvin net-ties NT2/NT8, NT3/NT9; SL_A direct to PGND. Page 3 mirrors. Input filters R25/R30 (R43/R48) 120R + 2200pF.

**Consequence.** Low-current torque accuracy and high-modulation sampling limits.

**v4 fix.** For a 24V board, 1mOhm 3W wide-terminal shunts (Bourns CSS2H-2512K-1L00F) at gain 20 (still ~82A range) doubles SNR per amp; add a third shunt/channel on phase A (INA240A2, or a 3-CSA driver like DRV8353RS) so any two phases can be sampled regardless of modulation state.

### sensing-6 — DRV8301 DC_CAL pins left floating on both motor cells (minor)

**Claim.** The DC_CAL nets were "removed" in v3.5 by simply not connecting the sheet-symbol port: U4 pin 12 and U5 pin 12 float, relying solely on the DRV8301's internal pull-down. DC_CAL, when asserted, disconnects and shorts the shunt-amp inputs for offset calibration — a floating control line on a board full of high-dv/dt nodes risks momentary spurious loss of current feedback mid-commutation. (Offset calibration remains available via SPI, so the pin is not needed.)

**Evidence.** Page 1: both motor sheet symbols show the DC_CAL port unwired (EN_GATE beside it is wired); pages 2/3: pin 12 routes only to the dangling port. CHANGELOG v3.5 "Removed: DC_CAL signals".

**Consequence.** Best-practice violation; actual spurious assertion unlikely thanks to the internal pull-down.

**v4 fix.** Tie DC_CAL hard to GND at each driver (direct or via 10k), or delete the net and strap at the symbol; calibrate via the SPI CAL bits.

### sensing-7 — SO1/SO2 filtered only at the driver end; no local RC at the ADC pins (minor)

**Claim.** The 2200pF SO filter caps sit at the DRV8301 end of each motor cell (C38/C39 at TP6/TP7, C53/C54 at TP8/TP9) and the filtered SO nets reach PC0–PC3 (U2 pins 8–11) with no local RC at the ADC pins, leaving the sample-and-hold without a nearby charge reservoir. v3.5's 4-layer AGND plane already mitigates trace pickup, so this is a refinement rather than a demonstrated noise problem.

**Evidence.** Pages 2/3: cap placement as above; page 1: M0/M1 SO nets enter U2 pins 8–11 with no series R or C at the MCU end.

**Consequence.** Slightly degraded sampling robustness.

**v4 fix.** Small local cap (22–47pF to AGND) plus ~100R series at each ADC pin; route pairs adjacently over the AGND plane; if v4 adopts buffered/external CSAs (sensing-1), place the final anti-alias pole at the MCU end.

## 4. Power Rails

### power-rails-1 — Entire logic supply hangs off the M0 gate driver's integrated buck (critical)

**Claim.** All control power derives from U4's integrated buck (DCBUS → PVDD2 pins 53/54 → PH 50/51 → D1/L2 22uH → C29 68uF → 5V → U3 → VCC 3.3V and U8 → AVCC); U5 on page 3 has no buck components. Both U4 and U5 have VDD_SPI (pin 49) fed from VCC, i.e. from U4's own buck output, so a buck brownout also corrupts the SPI path used to configure/clear faults on *both* gate drivers. When a shorted M0 FET destroys U4 — the most common documented ODrive field failure — the MCU, CAN, the power LED, the AUX brake control, and M1's command/SPI interface all lose power while the unfused DC bus stays energized; M1's DRV8301 retains PVDD but its inputs go dead, so both motors coast with no supervision or braking.

**Evidence.** Page 2: buck components as listed, pin 49 VDD_SPI on VCC. Page 1: 5V feeds U3 (VCC), U8 (AVCC), J4/J2 5V pins, C65. Page 3: no buck components — the 5V rail exists only through U4.

**Consequence.** The single most plausible component failure removes all supervision and brake-chopper control on a live, unfused bus.

**v4 fix.** Dedicated wide-Vin synchronous buck from DCBUS for the 5V rail, independent of any gate driver — LMR36015/TPS54360B (60V) for the 48V build, LMR51430 for 24V — with its own input RC/ferrite. With the move to DRV8353RS (web-5), do not use its integrated buck for system power (or diode-OR it as a redundant source). This also lets the MCU log/annunciate a gate-driver failure instead of going dark.

### power-rails-2 — No voltage supervisor; U4 PWRGD unconnected; STM32 BOR not guaranteed (major)

**Claim.** No brownout supervision exists: U4 PWRGD (pin 4) is a bare stub, there is no reset supervisor on nRST (net goes only to J2 pin 5 and U2 pin 7), and the STM32F405 ships with BOR disabled (POR/PDR fallback ~1.7V). During a VCC sag the MCU can execute erratically while the DRV8301s remain operational down to PVDD = 6V and follow whatever appears on INH_x/INL_x/EN_GATE. The DRV8301's internal dead-time/cross-conduction protection rules out true shoot-through, but uncommanded, erratic motor output during a brownout is plausible, and firmware has no way to detect buck degradation before losing power.

**Evidence.** Page 2: PWRGD stub, no net. Page 1: no supervisor/reset IC; the only bus monitoring is the VBUS_S divider — useless once VCC itself sags.

**Consequence.** Uncommanded/erratic PWM and torque during brownout, with no advance warning.

**v4 fix.** 3.3V reset supervisor on VCC driving NRST (TPS3840DL30 or STM6824); set/document BOR Level 3 in production option bytes; route PWRGD (open-drain, 10k pullup to VCC) to a spare MCU input so firmware can pre-emptively disable EN_GATE and the AUX bridge on power-fail warning.

### power-rails-3 — 5V rail exposed on user headers with no per-port current limiting (major)

**Claim.** The buck's 5V output is wired straight to user-facing pins — J4 pin 2 and pin 8 (each directly adjacent to encoder signal/3.3V pins) and the J2 pin-1 5V added in v3.5 — with no polyfuse, load switch, or series resistance anywhere between C29 and the connectors. A shorted or misaligned encoder cable drives the U4 buck into current limit, collapsing 5V, VCC, and AVCC for the entire controller while both power stages and the DC bus stay energized.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J4/J2 5V nets wired straight through; no PTC/fuse/load switch exists between C29 (page 2) and any connector; the board has no fuse anywhere.

**Consequence.** A common external wiring mistake (hot-plugging/misaligning 0.1in headers) brown-outs the whole controller mid-operation.

**v4 fix.** Resettable PTC (0.5A MF-MSMF050-2) or better a current-limited load switch (TPS2553DBV, ~600mA, fault flag to a GPIO) between the internal 5V rail and each connector 5V pin, plus 10uF local ceramic per port.

### power-rails-4 — Buck soft-start pin SS_TR floating (minor)

**Claim.** U4 pin 56 (SS_TR) floats with no soft-start capacitor, unlike the otherwise fully populated buck configuration. Startup ramps C29 (68uF) plus downstream capacitance at essentially minimum soft-start time, drawing an inrush pulse bounded by the buck's internal current limit at every power-up, with some risk of 5V overshoot into the LDOs. Thousands of fielded boards start fine — this is bounded, not unbounded, inrush.

**Evidence.** Page 2: SS_TR bare stub (EN_BUCK likewise floating) while the compensation network (R14 47K, C24 82pF, C27 270pF, R15 220k RT_CLK) and C28 BST_BK show deliberate configuration elsewhere.

**Consequence.** Repeated current-limit startup stress, potential 5V overshoot.

**v4 fix.** 10–22nF X7R from SS_TR to GND (few-ms ramp per the datasheet SS equation), or, with power-rails-1, a discrete regulator with fixed internal soft-start (LMR36015: 4ms).

### power-rails-5 — 5V rail filtered by one electrolytic; buck ripple reaches AVCC and the ADC reference (minor)

**Claim.** The buck output node has only C29 68uF (polarized; the sole MLCC on the whole 5V net is C65 2.2uF on the Top sheet), so ~530kHz switching ripple across C29's ESR rides on the 5V rail feeding U8. U8's AVCC output has only C21 4.7uF, LDO PSRR at 500kHz is minimal, and AVCC is both the STM32 VDDA (C22 100nF only) and the DRV8301 REF pin (U4 pin 24) setting shunt-amp offset — coupling buck ripple into phase-current and VBUS measurements at the few-LSB level.

**Evidence.** Page 2: C29 sole cap at the L2/D1 node; R15 220k → fsw ~530–580kHz; U4 pin 24 REF on AVCC. Page 1: U8 fed from 5V, C21 4.7uF on AVCC, VDDA decoupled by C22 only.

**Consequence.** Several ADC LSBs of noise degrading the current loop.

**v4 fix.** 2x 22uF X7R 16V MLCC in parallel with C29 at the inductor output; ferrite bead (BLM21PG221) + 10uF between 5V and U8 Vin; proper AVCC/VDDA decoupling (1uF + 100nF at the VDDA pin per ST AN4073, 100nF at the DRV REF pin).

### power-rails-6 — VDDA decoupling is a single 100nF (minor)

**Claim.** VDDA (U2 pin 13) is decoupled by C22 100nF only; ST recommends ~1uF plus 100nF at the pin. The nearest bulk on the AVCC net is C21 4.7uF at U8's output elsewhere on the board (NT1 joins AGND to GND at U8 but is not in series with the AVCC trace — the issue is trace distance/inductance). With 12-bit conversions of 500uOhm shunt signals referenced to AVCC, the missing local bulk adds current-measurement noise.

**Evidence.** Page 1: C22 at VDDA, C21 at U8, NT1 net-tie; page 2: SO filters referenced to AGND.

**Consequence.** Additional ADC noise on the highest-sensitivity measurements.

**v4 fix.** 1uF X7R + 100nF directly at VDDA (and VREF+ if broken out), on the AGND island next to the pin.

### power-rails-7 — Buck operating point thin at high line; no DCBUS clamp at PVDD2 (minor)

**Claim.** R15 = 220k sets the buck to ~536kHz; on the shared 48V build (56V in, 5.16V out via R16 18k/R20 3.3k) required on-time ~170ns leaves thin margin over the ~130ns minimum controllable on-time, inviting pulse-skipping ripple at max bus voltage. On this 24V variant the on-time margin is fine (~390ns) and the DRV8301's 60V abs-max is not the binding limit — the 30V FETs avalanche first. The shared weakness that does apply here is PVDD2/DCBUS having no TVS or clamp while the brake resistor is optional.

**Evidence.** Page 2: R15 220k RT_CLK, R16/R20 feedback (Vout = 5.16V), PVDD2 direct to DCBUS with C25/C60 only; no TVS anywhere; brake resistor optional (page 4).

**Consequence.** For the 48V build: marginal buck operation at max line. For 24V: inherited unclamped-bus exposure only.

**v4 fix.** Drop fsw to ~300kHz (RT ~390k) and resize L2/C29, or (preferred, with power-rails-1) a discrete 60V+ buck (TPS54360B/LMR36015). SMB TVS across DCBUS near the buck input (SMBJ54A on 48V, SMBJ26A on 24V); make the firmware OV trip mandatory-on when no brake resistor is configured.

## 5. Interfaces & Connectors

### interfaces-1 — J4 interleaves 5V, 3.3V and signals on an unkeyed header; one-pin misplug puts 5V on the 3.3V rail (critical)

**Claim.** J4's pin order (1=VCC/3.3V, 2=5V, 3=M1_ENC_A, … 7=VCC, 8=5V, 9=M0_ENC_A) places the 5V rail directly between the 3.3V rail and the encoder A inputs on an unkeyed, unshrouded 12-pin 0.1in header. An off-by-one plug of an encoder connector — the single most common user error on this header style — connects the encoder's 5V supply wire to the VCC pin, back-driving the 3.3V rail above the STM32F405's 4.0V abs-max VDD, or drives 5V into ENC_A which has no series resistance.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J4 "Rightheader" pins 1=VCC, 2=5V, 3/4/5=M1_ENC_A/B/Z, 6=GND, 7=VCC, 8=5V, 9/10/11=M0_ENC_A/B/Z, 12=GND. Encoder nets go to U2 with only 3.3k pullups (R9/R10/R11, R32/R33/R34), no series elements, no keying.

**Consequence.** Destroys the MCU and everything on VCC in plausible normal use.

**v4 fix.** One keyed, shrouded connector per encoder (Molex SL or JST-XH 5-pin: 5V, GND, A, B, Z); never expose both 5V and 3.3V on the same connector (if 3.3V must be offered, isolate it behind its own connector or a jumper). Put GND between power and signal pins so an off-by-one lands on GND, and add 100R series resistors in each encoder line for residual misplug cases.

### interfaces-2 — USB J1: no ESD protection or common-mode filtering (major)

**Claim.** USB data lines run from the connector through 22R series resistors straight into the STM32 OTG_FS pins with no TVS/ESD array and no common-mode choke; the shield ties hard to GND with no discharge network. USB is a human-handled connector on a motor drive with large dV/dt sources nearby — an ESD strike during plug/unplug or common-mode noise from the power stage can latch up or destroy U2, and causes the well-known USB disconnect problems under motor load.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J1 D−/D+ → R1/R3 (22R) → U2 pins 44/45 (PA11/PA12); no other components on the data lines; SHIELD/SH tied directly to GND (TP3). Noise-induced USB drops under motor load are widely reported on v3.x.

**Consequence.** Board-bricking ESD exposure plus documented field-reliability complaints.

**v4 fix.** USBLC6-2SC6 (or TPD2E009) at J1 on D+/D−, 5V TVS (PESD5V0S1BA) and ferrite/RC on VBUS; consider USB-C (with 5.1k CC pulldowns) — micro-B is also a known mechanical weak point on fielded ODrives; optionally a common-mode choke (DLW21SN900SQ2) between ESD array and MCU.

### interfaces-3 — CAN: no bus-fault tolerance, no TVS, no split termination (major)

**Claim.** U1 (SN65HVD232DR) is a bare 3.3V transceiver with no bus-fault protection (abs-max bus-pin range roughly −4V to +16V) on a machine whose harness carries 24V DCBUS; a miswire to battery, a large ground offset, or bus ESD destroys U1. No TVS or common-mode choke on CAN_H/CAN_L; termination is a single 120R (R5) via SW1 with no split-termination common-mode stabilization. CAN_H/CAN_L also appear on the unkeyed J3 at pins 3/4 — separated from VCC (pin 1) by one GND pin (pin 2), so a two-position offset is needed to reach the 3.3V rail.

**Evidence.** Page 1: U1 with CANH/CANL going only to R5 via SW1 and to J3; no protection parts on the CAN nets. Changelog v3.5: switched termination is the single resistor.

**Consequence.** Transceiver destruction by plausible harness faults on a 24V machine bus.

**v4 fix.** Bus-fault-protected 3.3V-IO transceiver (TCAN334G or TCAN1042HGV, ±58V bus-fault, ±12V common mode); NUP2105L dual-line CAN TVS at the connector; split termination (2x 60R + 4.7nF to GND behind SW1); a dedicated keyed CAN connector (JST-GH or 4-pin screw terminal) instead of pins near VCC on J3.

### interfaces-4 — Encoder inputs: single-ended, unfiltered, unbuffered, unprotected (major)

**Claim.** All six encoder inputs (M0/M1 A/B/Z) run from unkeyed J4 straight to STM32 pins with only a 3.3k pullup each — no RC filter, Schmitt buffer, ESD clamp, or differential-receiver option — while v3.5's RC-filter provision covers only GPIO lines. Encoder cables routed near 24V PWM phase wiring couple spikes causing quadrature miscounts and spurious index pulses (encoder-noise position errors are a documented ODrive v3 field issue; the analogous ERROR_ILLEGAL_HALL_STATE applies to hall inputs on the same unprotected header class). M1_ENC_Z additionally sits on PC15-OSC32_OUT, which ST specifies with restricted speed/drive as it shares the LSE pad — a poor choice for an externally-cabled signal.

**Evidence.** Page 1: encoder nets with only R9–R11/R32–R34 pullups; U2A pin list shows M1_ENC_Z on pin 4 (PC15-OSC32_OUT); RC options J5–J28 exist only for GPIO lines.

**Consequence.** Position-feedback corruption on a motor controller — miscounts and false index events near phase wiring.

**v4 fix.** Per line: 100–220R series + 1nF to GND (tau ~100–200ns, fine for ≤1MHz quadrature) into a Schmitt buffer (74LVC2G17/74HC14), plus a low-cap ESD array (TPD4E05U06 or PESD3V3L4UG) at the connector. Move M1_ENC_Z off PC15 to a normal FT pin. Ideally add optional differential-receiver footprints (AM26LV32 / MAX3097E) for RS-422 ABZ encoders.

### interfaces-5 — Live DRV8301 configuration SPI bus exposed unbuffered on J3 (major)

**Claim.** J3 pins 8/9/10 expose SPI_SCK/SPI_MISO/SPI_MOSI — the identical nets wired to SCLK/SDO/SDI of both DRV8301s — with no series resistors or buffering; only M0_nCS/M1_nCS stay on-board. Users routinely attach external SPI devices (e.g. SPI encoders) here, so an external slave contending on MISO, a shorted/dangling wire, or coupled noise can corrupt DRV8301 transactions. Because nCS is internal, an external device cannot arbitrarily address the DRVs; the realistic failure is corruption of MCU-initiated configuration transactions (OC threshold, CSA gain, PWM mode) or register readback — protection can be left silently misconfigured.

**Evidence.** Page 1: SPI nets go both to J3 pins 8/9/10 and into both MotorCell sheet symbols as SCLK/SDO/SDI, nothing in between.

**Consequence.** External wiring faults can silently corrupt the bus that programs overcurrent protection.

**v4 fix.** Separate SPI peripheral (or a buffered/isolated branch — 74LVC125 with direction gating and 100R series) for the external header, keeping the gate-driver SPI on-board. Minimum: 470R–1k series between J3 and the DRV bus, plus firmware periodic register readback-and-verify, faulting on mismatch (also covers SEU/noise corruption).

### system-6 — Basic EMC/ESD hygiene missing on every external interface (major)

**Claim.** No transient protection on any external interface: USB J1 has only 22R series (R1/R3) and no ESD array; CAN (U1 SN65HVD232 — no bus-fault voltage tolerance, though its bus pins are 16kV HBM rated) reaches J3 with no TVS or choke; both encoders' A/B/Z inputs on J4 have only 3.3k pull-ups — no series R, cap, or clamp — on cables typically bundled with phase wires. USB dropouts are a documented v3.x field complaint.

**Evidence.** Page 1: all three interfaces as described; no TVS device anywhere on the board.

**Consequence.** ESD and coupled-noise corruption across every user-facing port.

**v4 fix.** USBLC6-2SC6 on D+/D− plus an RC (1M ∥ 4.7nF) shield-to-GND termination instead of a hard shield tie; PESD2CAN/NUP2105L at the CAN pins and a transceiver upgrade (TCAN332 / SN65HVD230-class, or TCAN1042HGV if 5V is acceptable); 100R + 1nF RC plus BAV99/PRTR5V0U2X clamp on each encoder input, which also debounces Z-index against phase-wire crosstalk.

### interfaces-6 — No USB VBUS connection or detection (minor)

**Claim.** J1 pin 1 (VBUS) connects only to test point TP1 — no divider into a GPIO, and the OTG_FS VBUS-sensing function is unused (net VBUS_S is the DCBUS divider, unrelated to USB). Firmware cannot detect host attach/detach, hurting re-enumeration robustness after the noise-induced USB drops this board suffers, and the self-powered device presents its D+ pullup with no VBUS present, contrary to USB 2.0 requirements for self-powered peripherals.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J1 VBUS routes to TP1 only; no sensing divider or MCU connection.

**Consequence.** Poor re-enumeration behavior; minor spec non-compliance.

**v4 fix.** J1 VBUS through a divider (47k/22k) with a small clamp to a spare 5V-tolerant GPIO/ADC pin; firmware gates the internal DP pullup (soft-connect) and re-enumerates on VBUS changes. Combine with the VBUS ferrite+TVS from interfaces-2.

### interfaces-7 — SWD header J2: 5V adjacent to 3.3V, unprotected debug signals (minor)

**Claim.** J2 puts 5V on pin 1 immediately next to VCC (3.3V) on pin 2 of an unkeyed 6-pin header (the 5V pin is new in v3.5). A reversed or offset debug cable applies 5V to the 3.3V rail or to SWDIO/SWCLK/nRST; the debug lines have only 22R series (R2/R4) and nRST has no series element, RC, or ESD clamp. Debug headers are handled with the board powered. Rated minor rather than major because the exposure is bench-only.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J2 "Header 6" — 5V→pin 1, VCC→pin 2, SWCLK via R2, SWDIO via R4, nRST direct, GND. Changelog v3.5 "Added: 5V pin on J2".

**Consequence.** A slip with a powered debug cable can take out U2.

**v4 fix.** Standard keyed 10-pin 1.27mm Cortex-Debug connector (or a shrouded, polarized 6-pin); drop the 5V pin from the debug connector (VTref = 3.3V only); ESD array (TPD4E05U06) on SWDIO/SWCLK/nRST plus 10k pullup + 100nF on nRST.

### interfaces-8 — AVCC exposed on J3; GPIO_3/4/5 lack even optional series protection (minor)

**Claim.** The precision LDO output AVCC — the ADC supply/reference for all current and voltage measurements — is brought out on J3 pin 6 next to AGND (pin 7); an external short or noise injection there corrupts or collapses the analog reference of a running drive (current feedback dies while the power stage keeps switching). Separately, the optional 3.3k/82pF RC input filters added in v3.5 cover only GPIO 1, 2, 6, 7, 8; GPIO_3/4/5 (J3 pins 13–15) connect to PA2/PA3/PC4 with zero series impedance, filtering, or ESD, relying purely on 5V pin tolerance.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J3 pin 6 = AVCC (from U8 feeding U2 VDDA); RC networks J5/R52/C63 … J25/R56/C67 only for GPIO_1/2/6/7/8_FLT; GPIO_3/4/5 direct. Changelog v3.5: "Optional RC filters on step/dir/en and UART lines: GPIO 1,2,6,7,8".

**Consequence.** Analog-reference vulnerability plus unprotected user GPIO.

**v4 fix.** Remove AVCC from the user header (or feed the header pin from VCC through 100R with local decoupling). Give every exposed GPIO the same series-R/C footprint (populate 100R minimum) plus one 4-channel ESD array per header bank (TPD4E05U06).

### system-7 — Connector/switch ergonomics invite miswiring (minor)

**Claim.** J4's 5V pins sit directly adjacent to each encoder group (GND exists mid-row at pin 6 and at pin 12), so an off-by-one insertion on the unkeyed header applies 5V to an encoder channel; the middle phase-B screw terminal is unlabeled on both axes (silk reads "C", blank, "A"); SW1 combines BOOT0 (DFU/RUN) and the CAN 120R termination in one DIP block; and the DFU-mode pull-downs (R59/R60) protect only the AUX half-bridge — the motor-cell INHx/INLx lines rely solely on EN_GATE staying low.

**Evidence.** Page 1: J4 pinout; J3 with AVCC(6)/AGND(8) among CAN/SPI/GPIO pins; SW1 note "Switches closed by default" driving both BOOT0 (R8 47K) and R5 120R; DFU note with R59/R60 on AUX_H/AUX_L only. `v3.5_top.PNG`: "C [blank] A" phase silk, "DFU/RUN" beside "CAN 120R" on one DIP block. (v3.1 famously shipped swapped M0/M1 silkscreen.)

**Consequence.** Miswired phases, accidental DFU entry when toggling CAN termination, encoder misplug damage paths.

**v4 fix.** Label all three phase terminals A/B/C on both axes with M0/M1 adjacent to their groups; shrouded/keyed encoder connectors (or at least GND at both ends of J4 so off-by-one lands on GND); BOOT0 on its own recessed switch or a 2-pin "DFU" jumper physically away from CAN-term, distinctly silked; 10k pull-downs on all six INHx/INLx lines so DFU weak pull-ups can never bias the motor bridges even if EN_GATE is disturbed.

### web-8 — Encoder/GPIO inputs historically not 5V tolerant, still weakly protected (minor)

**Claim.** On v3.2 and earlier, GPIO 3/4 (encoder pins) were not 5V tolerant, so common 5V encoders destroyed the inputs; v3.3 added 5V-tolerant pins and changed encoder pull-ups from 1k to 3.3k. v3.x I/O still lacks dedicated ESD/overvoltage protection and filtering on encoder lines.

**Evidence.** This repo's CHANGELOG v3.3 (2017-06-17): "All GPIO pins are 5v tolerant"; "Encoder pullup resistance 1k → 3.3k, to be compatible with 2mA push-pull encoders". v3.5 schematic confirms the residual gap (only pullups, no clamps or filters; RC options cover GPIO 1/2/6/7/8 only).

**Consequence.** The destructive defect was fixed in v3.3; a robustness gap remains.

**v4 fix.** Series resistance plus ESD/TVS clamps rated for 5V miswiring on every user-facing I/O; 5V-tolerant or level-shifted inputs throughout; RC/Schmitt filtering on encoder channels for long cable runs.

## 6. Thermal

### system-5 — PCB-only cooling for 24 FETs; one NTC per cell as a board-temperature proxy; electrolytics in the hot zone (major)

**Claim.** Twelve dual-FET switch positions per board cool only through the PCB, with heatsinking left as an optional "enclosure style" exercise (v3.3 changelog); each cell's single 10k NTC (R12/R13/C23; R49/R50/C58 for AUX) is a board-temperature proxy that cannot resolve the hottest phase leg. The 8x470uF bank is mounted on the bottom side directly opposite the FET row (`v3.5_bottom.PNG`), thermally coupled to the FET array through the PCB — the board's hottest region heats its most temperature-limited components.

**Evidence.** Pages 2/4: one NTC divider per cell; CHANGELOG v3.3: "Fliped LDO and C29 to bot side, for better clearance on top side for enclosure style heatsinking"; `v3.5_top.PNG`: FET array spans the mid-line with no thermal hardware; `v3.5_bottom.PNG`: electrolytic row opposite the FET band.

**Consequence.** Thermal limiter tracks the wrong spot; electrolytic lifetime eroded by FET heat; continuous-current rating dependent on unspecified external heatsinking.

**v4 fix.** (1) Via-farms under every FET thermal pad to a dedicated bottom pour sized for a specified bolt-on heatsink; publish a reference heatsink + gap-pad (Bergquist GP3000S30). (2) One NTC per phase pair (6 per board, 0402 NTCs into a muxed ADC input), or at minimum one per bridge half. (3) 105C/10k-hour bulk caps (Panasonic FR) kept out of the FET thermal shadow. (4) Honest derating: print the no-heatsink continuous current on the datasheet.

## Not Broken — Keep

Things v3.5 does well that v4 should carry forward:

- **Kelvin-connected shunts via net-ties** (NT2–NT5, NT8–NT11 into SN/SP pairs) — proper 4-wire sense on 500uOhm parts; extend the same scheme to all three legs in v4.
- **Split grounds with deliberate net-tie joins** (GND/AGND/PGND, NT1 at the precision LDO) — the analog island discipline is sound.
- **4-layer stackup with dedicated GND/AGND planes** (new in v3.5) — and the GVDD-caps-to-GND fix (commit 3c5231c) shows the grounding lesson of the v3.4 errata was learned.
- **Parallel dual-FET switch positions** — a legitimate low-Rdson strategy; keep it, with per-gate resistors and symmetric routing.
- **Modular hierarchical schematic** (MotorCell reused for both axes, AuxHalfH as its own sheet) — clean structure that made this audit tractable.
- **Per-cell NTC provision** — right instinct, wrong count; multiply, don't remove.
- **DFU-mode pull-downs R59/R60 on the brake inputs** — correct hazard recognition; v4 should generalize it to all gate-control lines.
- **Cycle-by-cycle Vbus sampling intent** (the C1 270pF change) — the fast firmware trip is worth keeping *as the second layer* behind the new hardware backstop.
- **Optional RC filter footprints on GPIO** — the pattern is right; extend it to every exposed pin.
- **Precision LDO (U8) for AVCC** separate from the digital 3.3V rail — keep, with better decoupling per power-rails-5/6.
